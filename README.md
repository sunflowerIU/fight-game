# Himalayan Duel

A two-fighter arena game set on a temple terrace above the mountains.

Double-click `PLAY_GAME.cmd` to open it in its own window, or open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7 and press **F5**. The game starts on the title screen: **START GAME** → choose your fighter → **FIGHT!**

## The fighters

**AMIT** — street boxer out of Thamel. Quick hands, stubborn guard.
**JANAKI** — trained on the temple steps. Long legs, longer patience.

Pick one and the opponent takes the other. The opponent is the **CPU** by default; switch it to **HUMAN** for two players at one keyboard, where both sides pick freely and a mirror match is allowed.

## Controls

| Action | Player 1 | Player 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move | A / D | Left / Right arrows |
| Jump | W or Space | Up arrow |
| Punch | J | O |
| Kick | K | P |

**R** restarts after a knockout, **Esc** goes back to the character select.

A punch deals 10 damage, a kick 16. First to lose all 100 health loses.

## Difficulty

Only relevant against the CPU. It changes three things that matter more than raw speed:

| | Easy | Medium | Hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Time to notice a change | 0.36s | 0.18s | 0.07s |
| Chance of dithering | 42% | 18% | 4% |
| Chance of punishing your recovery | 5% | 38% | 82% |

Easy also swings from much too far out, so it whiffs; Hard waits until the blow will actually land, and backs off after a trade. Nothing in `characters/cpu_brain.gd` cheats — it reads the same public state a player can see and presses the same four buttons through the same intent the keyboard fills in.

## How the fighters move

Movement follows fighting-game conventions rather than platformer ones:

- Walking **toward** your opponent is faster than backing away, so spacing matters.
- A jump is committed the moment you leave the ground; you cannot steer in mid-air.
- Attacks have a wind-up, a short window that actually hits, and a recovery you are stuck in. They root you in place apart from a small lunge on the active frames.
- An attack pressed slightly too early is remembered for a moment and still comes out.
- Fighters only turn around while standing on the ground and not attacking, so nobody spins mid-swing.

## How the artwork stays planted

Each pose is a separate photo, and the fighter is drawn standing in a different spot in each one. `characters/*.tscn` records, per pose, where that artwork meets the floor:

```
idle_ground = PackedVector2Array(562, 1512,   845, 1512,   279, 1357)
#                                balance pt   leading shoe  trailing shoe
```

The balance point is pinned to the fighter's position, which is what stops the feet jumping when the pose changes or the sprite mirrors. Each shoe gets its own soft shadow; the trailing one is placed deeper into the arena, because the artwork draws it higher up the frame. The sprite also casts a real silhouette into the sunlight.

If you redraw a pose, re-measure those three points in any image editor (read the pixel coordinates off the shoes) and update the matching line.

## Audio

Everything in `audio/` is generated by `tools/make_audio.py` — two looping tracks built from a drone, a tabla-ish groove and a plucked pentatonic riff, plus the combat and menu effects. Re-render or retune them with:

```
python3 tools/make_audio.py audio
```

It needs `numpy`, `scipy` and `ffmpeg`. `autoload/game_audio.gd` owns playback: effects go through a small pool of players so hits can overlap, and music cross-fades between screens.

## Where things live

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `autoload/match_config.gd` | the menu's choices, carried into the fight |
| `autoload/game_audio.gd` | music and sound effects |
| `scenes/main_menu.gd`, `character_select.gd` | the two menus; `ui_kit.gd` holds their shared styling |
| `scenes/nepal_arena.gd` | the stage, camera and fight HUD |
| `characters/fighter_controller.gd` | movement, attacks, poses, ground contact |
| `characters/cpu_brain.gd` | the computer opponent |
| `tools/make_audio.py` | the music and sound effect synthesiser |

To change damage, reach or attack timing, edit the constants at the top of `characters/fighter_controller.gd`. Walk speed, jump height and the arena walls are exported on each fighter scene.

## Tests

With a Godot binary on your path, from the project folder:

```
godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/combat_smoke.gd
godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/input_smoke.gd
godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/pose_smoke.gd
godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/cpu_smoke.gd
godot --headless --path . res://tests/menu_smoke.tscn
```

`pose_smoke` reads the artwork back and fails if a recorded shoe row no longer matches where the picture actually stops, so a regenerated pose cannot silently leave a fighter floating. `cpu_smoke` puts each difficulty in front of a dummy and fails unless the harder levels really do land more. `menu_smoke` runs as a scene rather than with `--script`, because the menus need the autoloads and Godot only sets those up when a scene is running.

Blocking, throws and combos are still to come.
